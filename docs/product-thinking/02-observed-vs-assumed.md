# Observed vs. assumed
A UX audit starts a hypothesis. It does not prove one. This page keeps what was seen in the current Anthem app separate from what is believed but untested.
## Observed (from my audit of the current app)
- The Pharmacy screen can be reached by scrolling to the bottom of the Home screen or by tapping the Pharmacy icon in the navigation bar.
- It takes roughly 3 swipes to reach the bottom of the Home screen (device not recorded).
- In the Pharmacy tab, a list of current active prescriptions is not visible right away. A member has to open View All Prescriptions, which shows their complete prescription history.
- In the current Anthem app, no confirmation or status is shown after a refill request is submitted.
## Assumed (not yet tested with real people)
- Members with many prescriptions have to hunt for a list that shows the status of their medications.
- Without a clear indication of status, members may order too early.
- Without a clear indication of status, members may order too late.
- Because there is no confirmation after submitting, members may send a duplicate request or worry that it did not go through.
- Members with several medications are the most affected, and refill timing comes up often enough to matter. This is a group I chose, not one I verified.
- When members run out of medication, it is because of what the app shows and not other reasons such as insurance timing, pharmacy stock, or forgetting.
## Riskiest assumption
The last one. If other reasons dominate, a better interface will not change the outcome much. It is the first thing to test in usability sessions.
